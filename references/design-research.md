# Design research: establish a basis from themes and real websites

Read for new designs, full redesigns, or inspiration research. Investigate the current product before selecting visual language and technology. A technical case library cannot replace research into the current theme. Local repairs can reuse existing evidence without a fresh broad search.

Research how the six core methods fit this product and complement one another. Theme references inform content; method references inform surfaces, feedback, hierarchy, and dynamics. Combine both into a project-specific design instead of treating a category collage or one website as a universal target.

## What to search

Extract keywords from the industry, audience, content format, and page task. Search in languages relevant to the product and its sources. Use the [usage context](art-direction.md#identify-the-usage-context) to distinguish actual workflow references from a product's promotional website; each supplies different evidence. Derive search terms from ordinary user descriptions as well as relevant design vocabulary, without requiring the user to supply style names.

| Research axis | Sources to seek | Question to answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Similar tasks | Comparable products, competitors, page types, and real workflows | How is content organized, and how do people complete the task? |
| Related themes | Brands, institutions, publications, photography, or cultural projects in the field | What domain knowledge, imagery, vocabulary, and content rhythm are appropriate? |
| Design expression | Relevant fashion or contemporary brand sites, outstanding personal blogs, designer portfolios, independent magazines, experimental websites | Which composition, typography, image arrangement, or interaction relationships can transfer? |

For a photography blog, searches might include photographer journal or photography editorial. For clothing, consider fashion brand, collection, or lookbook. Adapt keywords to the task. Fashion sites and blogs are possible source types, not mandatory styles to imitate.

Prioritize original sites supplied by the user. Use search engines and the [discovery entry points](research-coverage.md#eight-discovery-entry-points) to find concrete pages. Directories support discovery; original sites support observation. Look beyond UI templates, screenshot galleries, and familiar technical demos.

## How to study a case

Open selected original sites and inspect relevant pages and states: first-viewport emphasis, content sequence, type scale, image ratios, density, navigation, narrow layouts, and applicable interactions. Static layout references do not always require source inspection. Consult author code or official technical material when a mechanism needs to be reproduced.

Compare what each example solves and which content conditions it depends on. Verify domain facts through credible primary sources. Separate brand claims, author experience, direct observation, and inference. Novelty, awards, or directory placement are discovery signals, not quality verdicts.

Comparable products mainly explain information and workflows. Art-direction references must also match the requested expressive intensity. For a frontier design brief, conventional marketing pages from established brands may be insufficient: find specific compositions, materials, or dynamic relationships worth adapting. A shared subject does not imply a shared aesthetic direction.

Keep a concise record in the project's existing design notes:

`Original page and access date → task relevance → observed feature → reason to adopt or reject → project region and state → expected appearance/change and acceptance method → unverified items`

Convert adopted findings into the [visual target and reference mapping](implementation-spec.md#visual-target-and-reference-mapping), then add actual implementation evidence. A source list, style vocabulary, or praise without a project destination does not establish that research informed the design.

Collect reusable content relationships and design principles, not entire web pages. A link grants no right to reuse images, fonts, or code. Check licenses and brand restrictions for assets actually transferred.

## When to stop

Stop when the main content structure, visual direction, and critical actions have a defensible basis suited to the product. Continue searching only for specific remaining gaps. There is no fixed site count, requirement to exhaust directories, or obligation to adopt newly discovered technology.

Reuse current, task-relevant project research and identify gaps. The skill's general case library alone does not satisfy that condition. If browsing is restricted or a source is inaccessible, proceed from available material and state the limitation without claiming a visit.

Write conclusions into the [specification](implementation-spec.md), then select implementation. Keep research and technical explanations in work records; product pages show information their users need. For research-only requests, deliver sources, comparisons, and transferable findings and stop there.
